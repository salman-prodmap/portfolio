Executive summary

I led an HR transformation workstream for a multinational organisation operating across 20 jurisdictions. Rather than replace the systems or introduce a new intake channel first, I proposed improving the processes behind the existing HR inbox. We started with changes of circumstances and built the solution across eight releases, using HR feedback, AI and deterministic logic to connect the work end to end.

The challenge

HR Operations handled thousands of monthly queries across onboarding, employee references, changes of circumstances and other topics. Requests crossed HR teams, engineering and individual system owners, with downstream implications for payroll and the Service Desk.

Changes to salary, manager, department and working patterns involved repeated data entry, separate systems, signing and filing. Follow-up emails could create duplicate requests. Budget constraints meant a wholesale system replacement would take too long to deliver value.

My contribution

I shaped the platform vision and phased roadmap, working daily with the Head of HR Operations and closely with engineering and system owners. The transformation team set itself the challenge of delivering working software every two weeks.

I combined product leadership, business analysis, service design and solution architecture. I evaluated models and prompts against anonymised real HR emails before the production integration, worked through reference-data matching and supported testing and operational readiness. I personally designed and wrote this deterministic algorithm for reconciling employee changes across separate email threads.

The approach

Our platform vision was to provide a world-class HR experience for colleagues. Integration and orchestration across the existing systems offered a practical route towards that vision within the budget.

Transform the processes first. Evolve the channels next. We retained the HR inbox, improved one process at a time and planned to extend that approach to other request categories. Later channel changes remained future stages rather than prerequisites for useful automation.

Key decisions

  • Keep the intake familiar. Improve execution behind the HR inbox before asking colleagues to change channels.
  • Test realistic requests before integration. Use anonymised email chains with multiple employees, corrections and effective-date changes to evaluate models and prompts.
  • Use AI and deterministic logic where each fits. AI interpreted emails and assisted manager-name matching using retrieved HR records. Levenshtein matching handled service-line values against approved reference lists when including those lists made prompts too large.
  • Keep approval with HR. AI prepares the request; an operator reviews and approves before the HR system is updated. HR review remains the final step before an approved change reaches the system of record.
  • Separate delivered capability from future ideas. Broader policy and jurisdiction-specific RAG support remained a future step. The delivered reference-data lookup and manager matching served a narrower purpose.

Eight releases

Release 1 Replacing Excel with a workflow

Before: HR read emails and tracked changes across spreadsheet tabs.

Released: Introduced a workflow to create, track, audit and report on a single change, such as a manager or salary change.

For HR: HR could test the fields and steps before more change combinations were added. Email handling and workflow creation still required manual work.

Release 2 Handling multiple changes together

Before: The first workflow handled one change type at a time.

Released: Allowed salary, manager and location changes for the same employee to be created and tracked together.

For HR: Related changes could stay in one request. Email intake and workflow creation remained manual.

Release 3 Reading emails and creating workflows automatically

Before: HR identified CoC emails and copied details into a workflow.

Released: Integrated with the inbox. AI classified relevant emails, extracted single or multiple changes and created workflows automatically; unrelated emails stayed in the inbox. Retrieved HR records supported manager-name matching, while deterministic reference-list matching handled known service-line values.

For HR: HR reviewed the prepared request. Updates to the HR system still needed manual handling at this stage.

Release 4 Connecting follow-up emails to the request

Before: A follow-up email could create a duplicate workflow.

Released: Used conversation IDs and a mapping table in the orchestration layer to attach follow-ups in the same email thread to the original workflow.

For HR: One conversation stayed connected. Separate threads could still create separate workflows.

Release 5 Updating the HR system after approval

Before: HR reviewed the workflow, then re-entered approved changes in the HR system.

Released: Enabled employee records to be updated directly from within the workflow after the HR operator reviewed and approved the request.

For HR: Removed the separate manual update while keeping approval with HR.

Release 6 Integrating document signing

Before: HR moved between workflow and signing software to send, download and attach documents.

Released: Integrated signing into the workflow, including manager-then-employee signing where needed. The signed document returned automatically.

For HR: HR reviewed and sent the letter from one place. Signatories still needed to review and sign.

Release 7 Integrating document storage

Before: Signed documents returned to the workflow but still needed manual filing.

Released: Integrated the document management system so signed documents were stored automatically.

For HR: Removed a separate filing step; HR could stay in the workflow.

Release 8 Combining requests across separate email threads

Before: Related changes for the same employee could arrive on different days in separate conversations.

Released: I personally designed and wrote this deterministic algorithm to track the employee and requested changes, distinguish repeats, additions and amendments, and create or update the workflow. Related changes were combined within a configurable time window; requests outside it started a new workflow.

For HR: Connected related changes across threads while retaining source emails and an audit record. HR review remains the final step before approved updates.

Outcomes

The CoC solution released 1,000 hours of annual capacity. HR could manage the request through one workflow, with less manual work across request creation, approved HR-record updates, signing and filing.

Same-thread follow-ups and related changes across separate threads stayed connected, reducing duplicate workflows. Reference-data matching supported consistent values, while audit trails retained the source emails and record of review and approval. Colleagues continued using the familiar HR inbox.

What I learned

Working software ≠ adoption

HR tested the early releases but wanted more functionality before adopting them in live operations. Delivering every two weeks did not mean HR could absorb a change to its ways of working every two weeks. We adjusted releases so the team could keep working while gaining improvements.

Use deterministic logic where it fits

AI request matching was inconsistent and required a growing history of earlier requests in the prompt. I worked through the rules with paper sketches and developed a deterministic algorithm over focused evenings. AI continued to interpret emails; the algorithm handled request matching.

Understand platform limits early

The workflow software made it difficult to highlight differences between source emails and proposed values for human review. Reporting mainly provided counts rather than insight into delays across jurisdictions and downstream teams. A custom-built solution could have given us more control over review and analysis.

Measure the whole request journey

A slow request might reflect a payroll handoff rather than the CoC workflow itself. Better reporting could reveal repeated corrections and manual steps, and inform the next area of transformation, employee onboarding.
